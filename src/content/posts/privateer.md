**[Play it in the browser](/privateer/)**: an alpha, on a desktop or a phone.
The run saves as you go, so you can close the tab and come back to it.

Plymouth, autumn 1580. Drake is home with a hold full of Spanish silver, and
every gentleman with a ship thinks the same thing: I could do that too. You are
one of them. Choose your ship, the way Oregon Trail has you choose banker,
carpenter or farmer: a pinnace with a fat purse and a weak deck, a sloop, or a
brigantine with guns and little money. Then choose whose money you sail on: the
Crown's, with stores from the Queen's own and a third of what you bring home
for her; gentlemen adventurers', who put in the most and take half your profit;
or your own, and keep it all. Take a gentleman-poet along if you like, to write
the voyage up if he lives. Fit her out at Plymouth, with chests of knives and
beads for trading as well as biscuit and water, sail round the world the
way Drake did, or a way of your own, take what Spain leaves lying about, and
come home to Plymouth rich, or not at all.

It borrows its bones from Oregon Trail. The voyage is a chain of landmarks on a
chart built from real coastlines, with forks where the captains of the time
chose: the Strait of Magellan or round the Horn, on across the Pacific or back
the way you came, the Moluccas or Manila. It is one screen and one button:
continue on, and the ship sails the sea lanes a day at a time until something
happens. Every day the hands eat and drink, faster in the tropics
and slower on short rations; six weeks without fresh food and the scurvy starts
to kill. At a landmark, water, forage or careen where there's a shore, buy from
a chandler or trade with islanders, or raid a Spanish town; on the way, meet
what comes: storms, calms in the doldrums, williwaws in the Strait, fever, foul
water, weevils in the bread, and a sail on the horizon. Prisoners, islanders and
chandlers tell you things, and a rumor followed opens a detour: the Manila
galleon off Cabo San Lucas, the Great Ship of Macau, a carrack from Goa off the
Azores, the Isles of Solomon. Some are true and some cost you the days for
nothing. A storm can drive you ashore where you never meant to go: Bermuda,
St Helena, the Galápagos.

Six of the hands have names: the master, the gunner, the carpenter, the
surgeon, the boatswain and the boy, each good for one small thing, and when
one dies the log says who and of what. Where they die they leave a grave, and
the next voyage that passes finds it. The peoples of the coasts remember you
too: deal fairly with the Miwok or the Tupinambá and they bring food and
guides; take what you need by force and the coast is shut to you, this voyage
and the next. Huguenot privateers out of La Rochelle will keep you company,
trade news, or give you a French commission. Where nobody holds the land you
can set up a post with the Queen's arms on a brass plate, as Drake did at Nova
Albion.

Every fight is cards, and the first one teaches you as you play; there's a
fishing boat at the Canaries to practise on. The ship you chose makes your deck, and the other
ship's go into the same deck. Each round three are dealt and you keep two,
which play in the order you pick them; played in the order of a boarding
action, sail then shot then board, each card hits harder than the last. A fight
spends powder and kills hands, a prize's hold restocks yours, and now and then
there's a jewel in her great cabin. Off Cape San
Francisco the treasure ship from Callao comes alongside, taking you for a
Spaniard, unless your raids on the coast have warned her. Spanish and
Portuguese prizes are lawful, and every one adds to what Spain will demand
back. A French or Hanseatic merchant is richer than she looks, and taking her
is piracy.

Coming home isn't the end of it. The backers take their shares, Spain's
ambassador demands restitution, and there is one last choice: show yourself at
court for the fame and risk being disowned to please Spain, lie low, or buy the
Queen's favour with a gift: jewels set aside for her, or a dead Spanish
commander's ring as proof of your service. With a name for piracy and nobody
at court behind you, the prize is seized and you are named a pirate. Then the
voyage is scored, treasure and hands and ship and fame, more for the smaller
ship, against the voyages of the time: Drake's, round the world, rich, with most of the crew;
Cavendish's, rich and dear in men; Fenton's, home and poorer; or Oxenham's,
lost.
